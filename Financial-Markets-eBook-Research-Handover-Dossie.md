## 1. Purpose and research standards

The eBook is a free, downloadable general-audience explanation of financial markets, with a strong Papua New Guinea focus. It is not a formal school textbook, government publication, investment prospectus, or personalised financial-advice service.

For every important claim or statistic, record its exact source, document title, publication date, reference period, unit, definition, and whether it is an actual result, estimate, provisional value or forecast. Never turn a forecast into an actual result, present old data as current, or invent source details. If evidence is unavailable or conflicting, state the limitation.

Prefer primary official sources: Bank of Papua New Guinea (BPNG), PNG Treasury, National Statistical Office (NSO), Securities Commission of Papua New Guinea (SCPNG), PNGX, relevant regulators, and official IMF, World Bank and WTO publications.

## 2. Financial-market fundamentals

Financial markets are systems and arrangements through which financial assets and claims are issued, bought, sold, priced or transferred. They connect savers and borrowers, help allocate capital, support price discovery, provide liquidity, and transfer or redistribute some risks.

Core concepts to explain in plain language:
- **Financial asset:** a claim such as a bank deposit, share or bond.
- **Saving:** setting money aside for security or a goal.
- **Investing:** committing money to an asset or activity with uncertain future outcomes.
- **Return:** income and/or change in an asset's value over a stated period.
- **Risk:** the possibility that actual outcomes differ from expectations, including loss.
- **Liquidity:** how readily an asset can be converted into money without a substantial loss.
- **Diversification:** spreading exposure to reduce some risks; it does not eliminate all risk.
- **Inflation:** an increase in the general level of prices.
- **Nominal return:** return before adjusting for inflation.
- **Real return:** return adjusted for purchasing-power changes.
- **Compounding:** returns generating additional returns over time; losses and fees also affect outcomes.
- **Price discovery:** the process by which buyers and sellers establish prices.
- **Capital allocation:** movement of funds to borrowers, issuers and investment opportunities.

Risk types include market, credit, liquidity, interest-rate, inflation, foreign-exchange, concentration, operational and fraud/scam risk. Never imply that markets guarantee wealth or that diversification guarantees profit.

## 4. BPNG and monetary policy

BPNG's primary monetary-policy objective is price stability, alongside broader responsibilities concerning financial stability, sustainable growth and development of the financial system.

Baseline findings preserved from prior research:
- March 2026 Kina Facility Rate (KFR): **5.0%**.
- March 2026 Cash Reserve Requirement (CRR): **9.0%**.
- BPNG's September 2026 outlook projected inflation at approximately **4%**.

The KFR and CRR are date-specific. The inflation figure is a forecast, not a full-year actual result. Verify all against exact BPNG releases before publication. A policy rate is not the rate every depositor receives or every borrower pays.

## 6. Foreign exchange and the kina

Explain foreign exchange, imports and exports, exchange rates, foreign reserves, and the effects of foreign-currency availability on households and businesses.

Research notes say IMF material described PNG's exchange-rate arrangement as **crawl-like**. They also record improved FX access during the period covered, associated with reforms, reduced kina overvaluation and stronger commodity prices. Recheck current conditions and do not claim FX shortages have permanently disappeared.

Foreign reserves are not government revenue, total exports, or money freely available for any spending purpose.

## 7. Commodity dependence and trade

Baseline research findings:
- WTO material reported energy and mineral products represented more than **77% of PNG exports in 2024**.
- WTO material reported a commodity-dependence index around **97%**.
- BPNG March-quarter 2024 merchandise exports: **K11,334.0 million**.
- Mineral products including LNG and condensate: **K9,958.5 million**, approximately **87.9%** of that quarterly total.
- World Bank material reported a current-account surplus around **15.1% of GDP in 2024**, driven by mineral exports after Porgera reopened and high copper and gold prices.

These are different measures and time periods. Do not combine annual WTO and quarterly BPNG datasets as though they were identical. Explain how commodity prices and production can affect export earnings, foreign exchange, public revenue and growth without implying all households benefit equally.

## 11. Financial inclusion and digital finance

The National Financial Inclusion Strategy covers **2023–2027**. Priorities include women, rural and mobile/digital services, financial literacy, consumer protection and data.

Research notes report BPNG/CEFI financial-literacy training reached more than **250,000 people**, including around **120,000 women**. Confirm the exact programme report, period and counting method.

No sufficiently reliable current national headline inclusion rate was established. Do not use “75% financially excluded” without verifying exact source, date and definition. Do not use approximately 32% deposit-account coverage without the same checks.

The research recorded approximately **1.47 million registered mobile-money accounts in Q4 2024**. Accounts are not necessarily active users, unique individuals or adults. Explain digital finance's benefits and risks, including fees, connectivity, fraud, impersonation, PIN security, privacy and dispute resolution.

## 13. Depositor protection and deposit insurance

Research confirmed depositor protection under banking law but did not establish a separate formal deposit-insurance scheme. The exact current legal position remains unresolved.

Do not write either “PNG has deposit insurance” or “PNG has no depositor protection” without authoritative evidence. Distinguish prudential regulation, depositor rights, lender-of-last-resort arrangements and a formal scheme that pays eligible depositors under defined conditions.

## 14. Cryptocurrency and virtual assets

BPNG warnings about unlicensed virtual-asset service providers and cryptocurrency investment platforms were identified. The complete current legal status was not resolved.

Do not claim cryptocurrency is categorically illegal in PNG or that all crypto activity is fully legal and regulated without authoritative current legal research. Distinguish warnings about unlicensed providers from a legal ruling on every type of activity. Explain volatility, custody/platform risk, scams and irreversible transfers.

## 15. Scams and consumer protection

Official warnings exist, but no reliable national scam-prevalence statistic was established. Do not invent victim counts or losses.

Teach warning signs such as guaranteed or implausibly high returns, urgency, fake institutional identities, requests for PINs/passwords/one-time codes, unverified investment platforms, advance fees to release supposed profits, and recruitment schemes presented as investments. Encourage independent verification and reporting through official channels.
