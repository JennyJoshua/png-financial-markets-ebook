## 6. Overview of Financial Markets

Financial markets connect people and organisations that have funds to save or invest with those that need to borrow or raise capital. They also allow investors to buy and sell existing financial assets, manage risk, and make payments.

### Core market types

| Market | What it does | PNG relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Money market | Short-term borrowing, lending, and debt instruments | Banks, BPNG operations, Treasury bills, and liquidity management |
| Capital market | Longer-term equity and debt financing | PNGX listings, shares, bonds, and corporate fundraising |
| Primary market | New securities are issued for the first time | A company issuing new shares or a government issuing new securities |
| Secondary market | Existing securities are traded between investors | Buying or selling existing PNGX-listed shares |
| Equity market | Ownership shares in companies are issued and traded | PNGX-listed company shares |
| Debt market | Loans, bonds, and other borrowing instruments are issued and traded | Government bonds, corporate debt, bank lending |
| Foreign-exchange market | One currency is exchanged for another | Kina–US dollar and other currency transactions |
| Commodity market | Physical or financial exposure to commodities | Minerals, energy, agricultural exports, and related price risk |

### Financial intermediaries

Financial intermediaries include banks, savings and loan societies, microfinance institutions, finance companies, superannuation funds, insurers, payment providers, brokers, and investment managers. They collect funds from savers, provide credit or investment services, process payments, and help households and businesses manage financial risk.

For ordinary readers, the practical functions are:

- Saving money safely and earning interest.
- Borrowing for a home, business, education, or emergency.
- Investing for long-term goals.
- Making and receiving payments.
- Protecting against loss through insurance.
- Saving for retirement through superannuation.

### Plain-English explanation

- The **Kina Facility Rate** is BPNG’s key policy interest rate. Changes can influence borrowing and saving rates across the economy, although commercial rates do not move automatically or identically.
- The **cash reserve requirement** is the share of certain deposits that banks must hold as reserves. A higher requirement can reduce funds available for lending; a lower requirement can increase liquidity.
- The **exchange rate** affects the kina price of imports, foreign debt servicing, travel, and export earnings.

The eBook should explain that monetary policy affects households indirectly and with delays. It should not imply that a change in the KFR immediately changes every bank’s interest rate.

## 21. Scams, Fraud, and Investor Protection

Investment scams often promise high or guaranteed returns, pressure people to act quickly, use secrecy, recruit new investors, or make withdrawal difficult. Common schemes include Ponzi schemes, pyramid schemes, impersonation scams, fake investment platforms, identity theft, and misleading advertising.

### Safety principles for the eBook

- Be cautious of guaranteed or unusually high returns.
- Check whether a person or company is licensed before investing.
- Do not rely only on social-media testimonials or messaging-app groups.
- Never send money or personal identification details to unverified contacts.
- Ask for written product documents and understand fees, risks, and exit arrangements.
- Keep records of payments, messages, contracts, and account details.
- Report suspected fraud to the relevant institution and authorities.
- Seek independent advice before making a significant financial decision.
