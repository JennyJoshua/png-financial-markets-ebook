2. Editorial Rules

Apply these rules throughout the ebook:

1. Explain financial-market concepts in plain language.
2. Introduce global concepts before applying them to PNG.
3. Use PNG examples wherever they improve understanding.
4. Avoid unnecessary technical jargon.
5. Do not provide personalised investment advice.
6. Do not imply that any investment is guaranteed.
7. Do not imply that diversification eliminates risk.
8. Do not imply that past performance predicts future performance.
9. Clearly distinguish:
   - fact;
   - historical fact;
   - estimate;
   - forecast;
   - interpretation;
   - unresolved claim.
10. Dates must accompany important statistics.
11. PNG-specific legal or regulatory claims should preferably come from official PNG sources.
12. Do not use an outdated statistic merely because it is widely repeated online.
13. Where current information cannot be verified, explicitly mark the item "NEEDS VERIFICATION".
14. Explain statistics in context rather than presenting unexplained numbers.

5. Core Financial-Market Concepts

The ebook should establish that financial markets are systems in which financial claims/assets are issued, bought, sold, exchanged, or priced.

Core functions:

- connecting savers and borrowers;
- allocating capital;
- price discovery;
- providing liquidity;
- transferring and managing risk;
- transforming maturities;
- supporting economic activity.

Explain the difference between:

- financial markets;
- financial institutions;
- financial instruments/assets;
- financial services.

7. Bank of Papua New Guinea and Monetary Policy

BPNG's primary objective is price stability.

Its broader objectives include:

- financial stability;
- sustainable economic growth;
- development of the financial system.

For March 2026, the research identifies:

- Kina Facility Rate (KFR): 5.0%
- Cash Reserve Requirement (CRR): 9.0%

These figures must be checked against the latest official BPNG information immediately before publication.

The research also identifies PNG's foreign-exchange arrangement as crawl-like, according to IMF reporting.

Do not treat any policy rate as permanent. Monetary-policy figures are time-sensitive.

9. Foreign Exchange

Foreign exchange should be explained through real-life PNG examples.

Relevant examples include:

- imported fuel;
- imported food;
- machinery and equipment;
- school/business supplies;
- exporter earnings;
- overseas payments.

The research indicates that FX access improved following central-bank reforms, reduced kina overvaluation, and stronger commodity prices.

Do not oversimplify this into a claim that FX shortages have permanently disappeared.

10. Commodity Dependence

Commodity dependence should be one of the central themes of the book.

The research identifies WTO evidence that:

- energy and mineral exports represented more than 77% of PNG exports in 2024;
- the export commodity-dependence index was approximately 97%.

BPNG data for the March quarter of 2024 identified merchandise exports of approximately K11,334.0 million, of which mineral exports including LNG and condensate were approximately K9,958.5 million, or about 87.9%.

These figures must retain their dates and definitions.

Major PNG exports include:

- LNG;
- crude petroleum;
- gold;
- copper;
- palm oil;
- coffee;
- cocoa;
- logs;
- other agricultural products.

The book should explain why commodity dependence matters to:

- government revenue;
- foreign exchange;
- investment;
- economic growth;
- employment;
- financial-market conditions;
- household/business conditions.

11. PNGX and Capital Markets

PNGX is PNG's only stock exchange.

It began in 1999 as the Port Moresby Stock Exchange and was renamed PNGX in December 2018.

The current PNGX listed-companies page used in the research showed 13 listed companies.

The list included:

- BSP Financial Group
- Kina Securities
- Credit Corporation
- PNG Air
- City Pharmacy
- Steamships
- Kina Asset Management
- NGIP Argmark
- Niuminco
- Newmont Corporation
- Santos
- Pacific Lime and Cement
- Adyton Resources Corporation

Adyton's listing date was identified as 23 September 2026.

Because listings can change, the current PNGX list must be checked again immediately before publication.

Important PNGX rules

Retail investors do not simply buy shares directly from PNGX.

They must use an authorised broker/dealer.

SCPNG research identifies:

- JMP Securities
- Kina Securities

as licensed brokers/dealers.

Market-cap warning

Do NOT use the old SCPNG figure of approximately US$20.3 billion from October 2021 as a current market-cap figure.

Do NOT use an undated K138 billion market-cap figure without verifying:

- date;
- definition;
- source;
- whether dual-listed foreign companies are included.

Total PNGX market capitalisation may include dual-listed foreign companies and therefore must not automatically be treated as equivalent to the value of PNG domestic/local corporate activity.

Mandatory PNGX research gap

Verify immediately before publication:

- current total market capitalisation;
- current turnover;
- number of listed companies;
- recent listing/delisting changes;
- retail investor participation if available.

14. Financial Inclusion and Digital Finance

PNG's National Financial Inclusion Strategy 2023–2027 should be treated as an important source.

Research identifies priorities including:

- women;
- rural communities;
- mobile/digital banking;
- financial literacy;
- consumer protection;
- data measurement.

BPNG/CEFI research indicates that more than 250,000 people have received financial-literacy training, including approximately 120,000 women.

BPNG has also reported that more than 85% of the low-income population live in rural areas and lack access to formal financial services. This statement must retain its exact source, date, definition, and context.

Claims that must NOT be reused without verification

Do not state:

«"75% of Papua New Guineans are financially excluded."»

unless a dated, nationally representative source supports that exact claim.

Do not equate:

- registered mobile-money accounts;
- active mobile-money users;
- unique individuals;
- bank-account ownership.

Do not use the previously circulated figure of 1.47 million registered mobile-money accounts unless the original official source, date, and definition are located and verified.

17. Scams and Consumer Protection

The book should teach readers how to recognise financial scams without creating unnecessary fear.

Potential warning signs include:

- guaranteed high returns;
- pressure to invest immediately;
- requests to send money to unknown persons;
- unlicensed investment platforms;
- fake endorsements;
- requests for passwords/PINs;
- unrealistic cryptocurrency returns;
- pyramid/Ponzi-style structures.

Legal and regulatory claims about scams should be checked against current PNG authorities.
